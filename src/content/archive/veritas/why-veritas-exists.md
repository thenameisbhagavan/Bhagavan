## 1. Executive Summary

As AI systems assume greater autonomy, the "black box" problem becomes a critical engineering failure point. VERITAS was engineered to solve a specific problem: providing deterministic explainability and source attribution for generative AI outputs. This article explores why VERITAS exists and the foundational principles guiding its architecture.

## 2. Problem Statement

Generative AI models hallucinate. When deployed in enterprise or high-stakes environments, a model outputting a confident but incorrect answer is unacceptable. Users and engineers must be able to trace exactly *why* a model generated a specific token or sentence.

## 3. Why Existing Solutions Were Not Enough

Most RAG systems blindly append context to a prompt and hope the LLM uses it correctly. If the LLM hallucinates, there is no built-in mechanism to detect the hallucination, let alone prove to the user that the answer is grounded in factual evidence.

## 4. Design Goals

- **Traceability:** Every claim generated by the system must be cryptographically linked to a source document.
- **Explainability:** The system must provide a human-readable explanation of its reasoning process.
- **Fail-Safe:** The system must refuse to answer rather than hallucinate if sufficient evidence is not found.

## 5. System Architecture

VERITAS acts as a middleware validation layer between standard RAG retrieval and final generation.

Architecture Diagram: User Query -> Standard RAG Pipeline -> VERITAS Validation Middleware -> Claim Extractor -> Evidence Cross-Referencer -> Output 

## 6. Technology Stack

```text
Backend Framework: FastAPI (Python)
Validation Engine: Custom LLM Pipelines (GPT-4o)
Data Store: PostgreSQL, ChromaDB
Frontend: React
```

## 7. Component Breakdown

1. **Claim Extractor:** Parses the drafted LLM response into atomic claims.
2. **Evidence Engine:** Searches the original retrieved context for exact matches supporting the claims.
3. **Attribution Formatter:** Injects citations directly into the final text output.

## 9. Design Decisions

- **Post-Generation Validation:** Instead of trying to force the LLM to write perfectly cited text on the first pass (which often fails), we allow it to draft a response, and then run a rigorous second-pass validation step.

## 10. Trade-offs

- **Latency vs. Trust:** Adding a second-pass validation step doubles the latency of a standard query. We accepted this trade-off because in the domains VERITAS targets, accuracy is vastly more important than speed.

## 11. Challenges Faced

Defining an "atomic claim." Breaking a complex paragraph into discrete, verifiable statements required building a highly specialized prompt chain that consistently extracted testable facts without losing context.

## 12. Engineering Lessons

- **LLMs as Evaluators:** LLMs are significantly better at evaluating the factual accuracy of a statement against a given text than they are at generating a perfectly factual statement from scratch.

## 13. Performance Considerations

We optimized the second-pass validation by executing the claim verification checks in parallel asynchronously.

## 14. Security Considerations

- *Current Implementation:* VERITAS verifies data internally.
- *Future Enhancement:* Cryptographically signing the verified outputs to guarantee provenance in zero-trust environments.

## 15. Scalability

The validation middleware is stateless and horizontally scalable, limited only by the throughput of the underlying LLM API.

## 16. Future Roadmap

- **Confidence Intervals:** Providing numerical confidence scores (e.g., 95% verified) based on the semantic density of the supporting evidence.

## 17. Conclusion

VERITAS demonstrates that AI systems do not have to be black boxes. By introducing rigorous, multi-stage engineering pipelines, we can force generative models to adhere to strict evidentiary standards.
