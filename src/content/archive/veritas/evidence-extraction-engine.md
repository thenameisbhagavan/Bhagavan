## 1. Executive Summary

You cannot verify a paragraph; you can only verify a specific claim. The Evidence Extraction Engine is a specialized component within VERITAS designed to parse fluent, generated text and break it down into an array of atomic, testable propositions.

## 2. Problem Statement

LLMs generate fluent paragraphs containing multiple intertwined facts. For example: "In 2023, Apple's revenue grew by 5%, primarily driven by Services, while iPhone sales declined." Validating this sentence as a whole is prone to error. The system must validate the 2023 revenue growth, the Services driver, and the iPhone decline independently.

## 3. Why Existing Solutions Were Not Enough

Traditional NLP parsers (like SpaCy) can identify subjects and predicates, but they lack the semantic understanding to isolate complete, context-independent claims from complex sentences containing pronouns and implicit references.

## 4. Design Goals

- **Atomicity:** Each extracted claim must contain only one verifiable fact.
- **Context Resolution:** Pronouns (e.g., "It grew by 5%") must be resolved to their proper nouns (e.g., "Apple's revenue grew by 5%") during extraction.
- **Speed:** Extraction must happen in near real-time.

## 5. System Architecture

The extraction engine operates immediately after the initial draft is generated by the reasoning pipeline.

## 6. Technology Stack

```text
Processing: Node.js
Coreference Resolution: Custom Prompting / SpaCy (hybrid)
Extraction LLM: OpenAI GPT-4o-mini
Validation: Zod
```

## 7. Component Breakdown

1. **Preprocessor:** Normalizes text and resolves obvious coreferences.
2. **Extraction Agent:** A heavily constrained LLM prompted strictly to output an array of single-fact strings.
3. **Filter:** Removes subjective statements or transitional phrases (e.g., "It is important to note that") which cannot be empirically verified.

## 9. Design Decisions

- **LLM-Based Extraction:** We opted to use a smaller, faster LLM (`gpt-4o-mini`) strictly for extraction via JSON structured outputs. It proved significantly more adept at handling complex grammar than traditional rule-based NLP parsers.

## 10. Trade-offs

- **Oversplitting:** Sometimes the agent splits a sentence into claims that are too granular, creating unnecessary work for the verification engine. We accepted this trade-off, preferring to over-verify rather than under-verify.

## 11. Challenges Faced

Handling implicit comparisons. If a sentence says "Revenue was higher than last year," the extractor must realize that verifying this claim requires knowing both this year's and last year's revenue.

## 12. Engineering Lessons

- **Few-Shot Prompting is Crucial:** Providing the extraction agent with 10-15 highly specific examples of complex sentences and their desired atomic splits drastically reduced extraction errors and prevented the LLM from hallucinating new claims during the extraction phase.

## 13. Performance Considerations

We batch process paragraphs. Instead of sending the entire essay to the extractor at once, we split it by paragraph and extract claims concurrently.

- *Current Implementation:* N/A - operates purely on generated text.

## 15. Scalability

The use of `gpt-4o-mini` allows this step to be highly scalable and cost-effective, running as a stateless serverless function.

## 16. Future Roadmap

- **Local SLM Extraction:** Replacing the OpenAI API call with a locally hosted, fine-tuned Small Language Model (SLM) dedicated exclusively to atomic claim extraction to reduce latency and cost to zero.

## 17. Conclusion

The Evidence Extraction Engine is the vital bridge between fluid generation and rigid verification. By ensuring that only atomic, context-resolved claims are passed downstream, VERITAS can guarantee high-fidelity fact-checking.
