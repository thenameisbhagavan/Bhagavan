## Abstract

Since the proliferation of the microprocessor, the technology industry has operated within strict boundaries of scarcity: computational limits, memory constraints, and the finite supply of human cognitive labor. The compensation structure of software engineering has reflected this extreme scarcity of high-level systems design capability. This analysis models the macroeconomic implications of commoditized intelligence via Artificial General Intelligence (AGI) and its transition toward post-scarcity software economics.

## 1. The Deflationary Dynamics of Artificial Intelligence

Historically, software has functioned as a massive deflationary force on traditional industries, automating logistical and administrative overhead. Paradoxically, the production of software itself has remained intensely inflationary, characterized by escalating engineering compensation and resource costs.

Agentic systems fundamentally invert this dynamic. When a user can provide semantic intent to an AI orchestration layer, and the system autonomously provisions databases, synthesizes code, and executes continuous deployment protocols within minutes, the fundamental economic model of the SaaS industry faces collapse. Software transitions from a subscription-based commodity to a dynamically generated utility.

<technical-callout type="EXPERIMENTAL" title="Zero Marginal Cost Computation">
When AGI is capable of replicating the architectural output of a senior staff engineer at computational costs nearing $0.01 per hour, the marginal cost of software production asymptotically approaches zero.
</technical-callout>

## 2. The Reallocation of Cognitive Labor

If the economic cost of building software drops to zero, the role of the software engineer requires a fundamental ontological shift. In a post-scarcity paradigm, the engineering focus pivots away from mundane systemic optimization. The manual synthesis of CSS breakpoints or the tedious resolution of database deadlocks becomes fully automated.

The human engineer is subsequently freed to address complex, non-linear problems that are currently deemed economically unviable. The objective transitions from cost-reduction engineering to macro-scale scientific engineering.

The telos of artificial intelligence in the engineering sector is not the obsolescence of the human developer, but the automation of syntactical drudgery, unlocking human cognitive labor to construct the fundamental systems of the future.
